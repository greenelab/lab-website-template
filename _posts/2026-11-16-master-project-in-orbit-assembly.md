Large structures in space, such as telescopes, antennas and habitats, cannot be launched in one piece. They have to be assembled in orbit, by robots, with little or no human intervention and with very limited communication with the ground. This project studies the perception side of that problem: how a robot builds and maintains an understanding of the structure it is assembling.

**What you will work on**

- A perception pipeline that tracks modules and connection points during assembly, under harsh lighting and with specular, textureless surfaces.
- A representation of the partially assembled structure that stays consistent as modules are added, and that supports planning the next step.
- Evaluation in simulation and, where possible, on a robot arm in the lab with mock-up modules.

**What we look for**

- An MSc student in robotics, aerospace, computer science or a related field.
- Background in 3D computer vision or SLAM, and comfort with Python or C++ and ROS.
- Interest in bringing a method all the way to a working system, not only to a benchmark number.
